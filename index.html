The idea started with a conversation

Before the snow. Beyond the storm.

Creator Seymore Green overheard a Village Market manager as neighbors rushed to get supplies ahead of a major snowstorm. What if seniors and other residents could get essentials delivered without leaving home, while neighborhood runners earned money and shops served more customers?

Local Run Rozzie proposes that everyday connection: groceries, meals and local purchases delivered by familiar faces. Storm-preparation runs would happen only before conditions become unsafe; service can pause as weather requires.

Two local residents. One local business.

Customers and runners must live in Roslindale. Businesses must operate in Roslindale. Delivery addresses must be inside the approved pilot zone. Eligibility would be checked during onboarding.

Start tight. Grow smart.

Proposed pilot: pickups near Roslindale Village and nearby residential deliveries. Confirm each address before accepting a run.

  • Village core: Washington, South, Poplar & Corinth Streets
  • Expand toward Belgrade Avenue and Cummins Highway as demand supports it
  • Schedule lunch, dinner and shop-pickup windows
6 shops × 5 orders = 30 deliveries

A planning scenario—not proven demand. At 3 deliveries per hour, 30 takes 10 hours; at 4 per hour, 7½ hours. Pickup readiness and distance decide the pace.

The delivery needs its own funding.

A separate delivery fee or business subsidy must cover the gap. A $7 resident delivery fee could fund the $7 runner payment; the commission then funds operations before payment-processing costs, refunds and overhead. Final resident fees are not set.

No automatic volume thresholds invented. Pharmacy participation would require an agreed delivery protocol; prescription delivery is not enabled in this pilot.

The opportunity

Six first calls. Room for more.

Recruit local businesses that want to participate; the six examples are not an exclusive list. A next-stage target of 15–25 participating shops is a planning goal, not a verified count of available partners. Restaurants, bakeries, grocers, florists and retail shops could share one neighborhood delivery network.

Prospects to research next include Fornax, Delfino, Sophia's Grotto, Blue Star, Chilacates and Village Sushi. Confirm current operations and pickup suitability before outreach.

Useful tips for your first local run

Customers: confirm your address, pickup details and availability. Runners: confirm pickup readiness and handoff instructions before leaving. Businesses: keep orders labeled and ready for the agreed window.

Tips are optional and go to the runner under the proposed model. The calculator above shows base pay and tips separately. The saved app can show the presentation offline after your first visit; maps, email and outside links need a connection.
